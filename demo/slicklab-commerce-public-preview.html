01 / Final project blueprint

SlickLab Smart Commerce Orchestrator

A supervised commerce system that discovers viable products, publishes approved offers, accepts customer orders, validates fulfillment economics, controls procurement, tracks delivery and learns from actual profit.

02 / Goal

Build trust outside. Keep control inside.

Customer goal

A clean, familiar store with transparent prices, delivery estimates, protected checkout and simple order tracking.

Operations goal

A private command center for sourcing, approvals, suppliers, orders, exceptions and realized profit.

Business goal

Validate demand through controlled sourcing, identify winners, then move them toward direct suppliers and stronger margins.

04 / Components

Agents advise. Services enforce.

MARK-BOT

Compares landed cost, seller quality, stock, delivery and product risk. Recommends opportunities but cannot buy in V1.

WEB-BOT

Supports product discovery and order questions. It cannot change prices, payment state or refund state.

PROC-BOT

Rechecks supplier price and stock, finds alternatives and prepares a procurement request for approval.

REC-BOT

Tracks supplier orders, shipping events, exceptions, final costs and actual profit.

05 / Critical rule

Payment is not automatic buying permission

IF payment_verified
AND supplier_stock_confirmed
AND supplier_price_current
AND expected_profit >= minimum_profit
AND expected_margin >= minimum_margin
AND supplier_risk <= maximum_risk
AND address_valid
AND transaction_risk <= threshold
AND approval_policy_passed
THEN submit_supplier_order
ELSE hold_order_and_create_exception

03 / Deployment constraint

Do not install everything at once

P0
Use this browser prototype to validate the store, CMS and operational workflow.
P1
Deploy only storefront, commerce core, isolated PostgreSQL and MockPaymentProvider.
P2
Add Typesense and Novu after the catalog and order flow are stable.
P3
Add Windmill and approved Supplier Adapters for controlled automation.
P4
Expose narrow commerce tools to TCC/Hermes, then add analytics and selective automation.
VPS rule: production commerce must use isolated containers, databases, secrets, networks, backups and health checks. It must not share unrestricted database credentials with agents.
04 / Supplier abstraction

Never hard-code the business to one marketplace

SupplierAdapter
├── searchOffer(query)
├── getOffer(productId, variantId)
├── getPrice(offerId)
├── getStock(offerId)
├── getDeliveryEstimate(destination)
├── prepareOrder(order)
├── submitOrder(approvedRequest)
├── getOrder(supplierOrderId)
├── getTracking(supplierOrderId)
└── cancelOrder(approvedRequest)

Every adapter enables only operations permitted by that supplier or platform.
Research basis: Medusa, Vendure and Saleor were compared as open-source commerce cores; Typesense and Meilisearch as search options; Keycloak and other CIAM systems for identity; Novu and listmonk for notifications; Windmill and Temporal for workflows; Metabase and PostHog for analytics; and official Shopee/Lazada Open Platforms for marketplace integration limits. Final production licensing and current API eligibility must be revalidated immediately before implementation.
